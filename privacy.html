Baby Steps — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 5 September 2026

Baby Steps is an app for keeping track of a child's health: doctor visits, vaccinations, medications, growth, teeth, symptoms, the papers a clinic hands you, and reminders.

This policy describes what happens to the information you put into it. It is short because the app does very little with your data — by design.

The short version

  • Everything stays on your phone. Baby Steps stores your records in a database on the device and nowhere else.
  • There is no account. You do not sign up, sign in, or give us an email address.
  • We do not collect anything. No analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising identifiers, no usage tracking.
  • Nothing is sent to a server. Baby Steps has no server.
  • We never sell or share your child's health data, because we never receive it.
  • There are no ads.

What Baby Steps stores, and where

Everything you enter is written to a private database inside the app's own storage on your device. Both platforms protect that storage so other apps cannot read it: Android through app-private storage, an iPhone through the app's sandbox.

What is stored:

We do not receive any of it. It is on your device, in your control.

What Baby Steps does not do

  • It does not create an account or a profile on any server.
  • It does not upload, back up or synchronise your data anywhere, and it switches off the operating system's own backup so that the phone cannot do it either: Android's automatic backup to Google Drive is disabled for this app, and on an iPhone the records are excluded from iCloud and from encrypted computer backups. Your child's records are not copied anywhere — not even by the operating system.

This cuts both ways, and you should know which way. Because nothing is backed up, a lost or reset phone takes the records with it. The app's Export everything (More → Data and privacy) is how you keep a copy, and it is the only way there is.

  • It does not use analytics or crash-reporting services.
  • It does not contain advertising, and does not use health data for marketing.
  • It does not read your contacts, your photo library, your location or your microphone. On an iPhone it asks for the camera, and only to take a photo you asked it to take — see Permissions below.

Permissions, and why each one exists

Baby Steps asks for as little as it can.

Permission Where What it is for
Notifications Android and iPhone To show medication and vaccine reminders.
Alarms and reminders (exact alarms) Android only So a dose reminder arrives at the dose time rather than whenever the system gets round to it. iPhones have no equivalent setting; a reminder there arrives at the time it was set for.
Run at startup Android only Reminders are cleared by a restart. Without this, restarting your phone would silently stop your reminders.
Vibrate Android only Part of how a reminder notification is delivered. Added by the notification library the app uses.
Camera iPhone only Asked for the first time you tap Take a photo, and only then. See below — this is the one place the two platforms genuinely differ.
Time-sensitive notifications iPhone only So a dose reminder is delivered while a Focus — including Sleep — is switched on. Not a prompt: it is granted with notifications, and you can switch it off per app in Settings.

Baby Steps never has access to your photo library, on either platform, and never asks for it.

When you add a photo — an attachment on a visit or vaccination, or a profile picture for your child — you can either pick an existing one or take a new one:

  • Choosing an existing photo opens the phone's own photo picker — Android's photo picker, or the iPhone's Photos picker. Both run outside the app and hand it the single file you chose and nothing else. Neither needs, or asks for, permission to your library. The app also deliberately declines the picture's embedded metadata, so the location the photo was taken at is not copied in with it.
  • Taking a photo differs, and this policy would rather say so than round it off:
  • On Android, it opens your phone's own camera app, which returns just the photo you took. Baby Steps never sees the camera, which is why no camera permission is asked for and none is declared.
  • On iPhone, the camera runs inside the app, and iOS requires your permission for that. You will be asked once, the first time you tap Take a photo, and you can refuse — everything else in the app keeps working, and you can still attach a photo you have already taken. The camera is opened at no other moment.

Either way, only that one file is copied into the app's own storage. A profile picture is scaled down to at most 512 pixels on its longest side before it is copied, because it is only ever drawn small.

Everything copied in is included when you export, and removed when you delete the child it belongs to or delete everything.

Children's data

Baby Steps holds health information about children, which both Egypt's Personal Data Protection Law (No. 151 of 2020) and the GDPR treat as sensitive.

The app is used by a parent or caregiver, not by the child, and the records are entered by that adult about their own child. Because nothing leaves the device, that information is never processed by us or by anyone else.

Your data is yours

From More → Data and privacy you can, at any time and without asking anyone:

  • Export everything. Every record the app holds, as a single file you can keep or send on. The file is not encrypted once it leaves the app, so keep it somewhere you trust.
  • Delete everything. Permanently removes every record for every child. There is no copy anywhere else, so this cannot be undone and we cannot restore it for you.

You can also remove a single child's records from their profile.

Uninstalling the app also removes its data from your device.

Medical disclaimer

Baby Steps is an organiser, not a medical device and not a source of medical advice.

  • Vaccine dates come from your country's schedule as reported to the World Health Organization, and growth charts use the WHO Child Growth Standards. Both are shown with their source.
  • Reminder timing, and the suggestion of kinder dose times, are the app's own scheduling convenience — not medical guidance, and labelled as such where they appear.
  • Percentiles describe where a child sits among children of the same age. They are not a score and not a diagnosis.
  • Always follow your doctor's instructions. If you are worried about your child, contact your doctor rather than relying on this app.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes materially, the app will ask you to read and accept the new version rather than quietly updating it. Your previous acceptances remain visible under More → Data and privacy.
